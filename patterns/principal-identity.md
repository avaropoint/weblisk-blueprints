# Principal Identity Pattern

Separates the three things a system confuses at its peril: an **identity**, which
says who a subject is and grants nothing; a **credential**, which binds that
identity to one hub on one device; and a **grant**, which says what the holder
may do there. Each has its own lifecycle, its own revocation, and its own blast
radius.

## Overview

`protocol/identity` specifies one ML-DSA-65 key pair per entity, registered by
public key with an orchestrator. `architecture/admin` specifies operator
registration against a single orchestrator, with the first operator admitted by
presenting the bootstrap secret that orchestrator emitted (see *Bootstrap is
trust-on-first-use* below). Neither answers what happens when one subject works across
several hubs, and neither separates *being someone* from *being admitted
somewhere*.

That separation is this pattern. Three layers, three lifecycles:

| Layer | Answers | Held by | Revoking it |
|-------|---------|---------|-------------|
| **Identity** | who is this subject | the subject, on their own machine | ends the subject everywhere |
| **Credential** | is this key that subject, at this hub, on this device | the subject's device; public half at the hub | ends one device at one hub |
| **Grant** | what may they do here | the hub | ends access, keeps the credential |

A subject holds ONE identity. It enters a hub with a credential **attested by**
that identity, and it acts there under a grant. The identity's private key is
used to mint credentials and for nothing else — it is cold. Day-to-day
authentication uses the credential, which is hot, scoped to one hub and one
device, and cheap to replace.

A **subject** may be a person or a service. Both are identified the same way,
bound the same way, and revoked the same way. The difference is operational —
whether a passphrase can be prompted for — not structural.

A credential may take either of two forms: a **key file** held by the subject's
tooling, or a **passkey** held by an authenticator (W3C WebAuthn). A passkey is
already a distinct key pair per relying party, generated on the device and
non-exportable — the credential layer's shape, implemented in hardware. Both
forms are attested by the identity the same way, and a hub authenticates them
the same way.

## Design Principles

1. **An identity is a key pair generated by its subject** — The private key is
   created on the subject's own machine, by the subject's own tooling
   (`weblisk identity init`), and MUST NOT leave it. No platform, control plane
   or hub may generate an identity on a subject's behalf. A platform that
   generates a subject's private key is a key escrow for every identity it
   created, however briefly it held the material.

2. **Identity confers nothing** — Holding an identity makes a subject
   identifiable, not admitted. Every capability comes from a grant issued by a
   hub, exercised through a credential that hub attested. Identity is the
   subject of an authorization decision, never the decision.

   The authority to issue a grant is written `grant:issue` in this pattern. It
   is the pattern's name for that authority, not a capability name in
   [`protocol/types`](../protocol/types.md)' list: no manifest declares it and
   no token carries it under that name. A blueprint that applies this pattern
   says which of its own capabilities carries it, and at a Weblisk hub that is
   `admin:*`, which admits operators and sets their roles
   ([`architecture/admin`](../architecture/admin.md#operator-roles)).

3. **A subject is a person or a service, treated identically** — Service
   accounts are identities. Same algorithm, same binding, same revocation, same
   audit. They differ only in `kdf`: a person's key is never stored
   unencrypted, and a headless service key MAY use `kdf: none` with file
   permissions as its sole protection, in development only (`protocol/identity`
   Key Categories, rules 1 and 6). Nothing else about the model changes.

4. **A hub is entered by a credential, never by the identity** — A hub stores a
   credential's public key, not the identity's. The identity key is presented
   once, at attestation, to prove the credential belongs to the subject. It is
   never used for authentication and never required online thereafter.

5. **The binding is an attestation, and the hub verifies it once** — The subject
   signs a statement with its identity key: *this credential public key is mine,
   for this hub, for this purpose, until this date*. The hub verifies that
   signature against the identity public key recorded in the invitation, then
   stores the credential public key. Verification happens at issue, not at every
   login.

6. **A credential may be classical; an identity may not** — Authentication is a
   live challenge-response and cannot be forged retroactively: an attacker who
   breaks ES256 in ten years cannot log in ten years ago. Attestations and signed
   records can be forged retroactively, which is why `protocol/identity` mandates
   ML-DSA-65 for them. So a passkey (ES256 or EdDSA) MAY serve as a credential,
   and MUST NOT serve as an identity, sign an attestation, or sign any record
   intended to outlive the session.

7. **Revocation is granular below, total above** — Revoking a credential ends one
   device at one hub and leaves the identity, the grant and every other
   credential intact. Revoking an identity voids every credential attested by it,
   at every hub. Both MUST be possible; they are not the same operation and MUST
   NOT be conflated.

8. **Rotation cascades, and this is deliberate** — Rotating a credential is
   local and cheap. Rotating an identity invalidates every attestation that
   identity ever signed, so every hub relationship MUST be renegotiated: the
   new identity key announced to each hub under the old one, new attestations,
   new credentials. There is no overlap period (`protocol/identity` Rotation
   Rules) and no partial migration. A hub that verified the announcement keeps
   the subject's grant and the identifier it records for the subject, so what
   it recorded before and after names one subject. A subject that cannot sign
   an announcement, having lost the old key, is admitted afresh, by a new
   invitation, as a new subject. An identity-level event is expensive because
   it should be.

9. **A directory locates subjects; it does not vouch for them** — A registry of
   identities, operated by a control plane, a CLI keystore or a marketplace,
   answers "which subjects exist and where do I reach them". A hub MUST verify
   signatures against the key material IN ITS OWN RECORDS and MUST NOT consult a
   directory at authentication time. A compromised directory must not become a
   compromised hub.

10. **Never join identity on a name** — Email addresses, display names and
   usernames are coordinates. The same subject may appear under several of them,
   in several directories, simultaneously. Systems MUST join on identity or
   credential key material and MUST NOT treat an address as an identifier.

11. **A service subject has fewer powers, never more** — Principle 3 holds: a
    service is enrolled, attested and revoked exactly like a person. Where it
    differs, it differs by SUBTRACTION. It has no presence signal, so it can
    never satisfy user verification and MUST NOT be granted a scope whose policy
    requires it. It has no judgement, so it MUST NOT issue invitations, vouch for
    another subject, or provision another identity — a service that can mint
    service accounts makes every boundary one call deep. For the same reason it
    MUST NOT hold a capability `protocol/types` marks *person only*. And it has
    no one watching it, so it MUST carry a named human owner and a mandatory
    expiry.

12. **Time is a security input** — Attestation validity, replay windows, token
    expiry and grant expiry are all decided by comparing clocks. A verifier MUST
    apply a bounded skew tolerance (SHOULD be ≤60 seconds), MUST reject
    timestamps outside it rather than clamping, and MUST NOT accept a
    not-yet-valid attestation. Where a host's clock cannot be trusted, security
    decisions MUST fail closed rather than proceed on an unverified reading.

13. **An agent is never a principal; it acts under one** — An agent has no
    identity, no credential and no grant of its own. It runs under a subject —
    a service, or a PERSON who has authorised it — and everything it may do is
    the intersection of three things:

        the subject's grant at this hub
      ∩ the agent's own declared capabilities
      ∩ what the subject authorised THIS agent to do on their behalf

    All three terms are required. Grant alone would let any agent inherit
    everything its subject can reach — connect a calendar once and every agent
    ever created acts as you there. Declaration alone is a promise the agent
    makes about itself, which is not authority. The third term is the subject's
    consent, and it is per-agent, per-capability, scoped and revocable.

    An agent MUST NOT widen its own authority, MUST NOT pass a capability to
    another agent that it did not itself receive, and MUST NOT act after the
    authorisation expires. An authorisation MUST NOT name a capability
    `protocol/types` marks *person only*, so the intersection never holds one,
    whatever the subject's grant holds. Where an agent acts for a person, the
    record MUST name both — the subject whose authority was used, and the agent
    and run that used it. An upstream system will show only the subject; the
    hub's ledger is the only place the causal chain exists.

## Contracts

```yaml
contracts:
  behaviors:
    - name: identity-generation
      description: Create a subject's durable identity
      required: true
      rules:
        - The key pair MUST be generated on the subject's own system
        - The private key MUST NOT be transmitted to any party, ever
        - A human subject's private key, wherever it is written or whatever secret source returns it, MUST be the weblisk-key-v1 envelope with kdf argon2id. A key that never leaves a secret source that signs has no such form (protocol/identity rule 1)
        - A service subject's key MAY use kdf none where no passphrase can be prompted, in development only (protocol/identity rule 6)
        - Only the public key is ever published

    - name: principal-invitation
      description: Admit a subject to a hub, binding the invite to a known key
      required: true
      rules:
        - The inviter MUST obtain the subject's identity PUBLIC key before issuing
        - The invitation MUST name that public key, the offered roles and scope
        - An invitation MUST be single-use, time-boxed and revocable before redemption
        - Issuing an invitation MUST require grant:issue at this hub
        - The offered roles and scope MUST NOT exceed the issuer's own. Authority
          is delegated, never invented — an issuer cannot offer what it does not hold
        - The grant created at redemption MUST be built from the invitation's
          offered roles and scope, and MUST NOT read roles, scope or any other
          authority from data supplied at redemption
        - Redemption MUST require a signature over a nonce bound to this invitation
          and this hub, within the 300-second replay window
        - An invitation MUST be refused if the subject already holds a grant here
        - If the identity key rotates before redemption, redemption MUST fail with
          identity_mismatch and the invitation MUST be reissued
        - The subject MUST be able to decline, and the decline MUST be recorded
        - The grant MUST record how the public key was obtained and whether it was
          verified out-of-band

    - name: credential-attestation
      description: Bind a per-hub, per-device credential to an identity
      required: true
      rules:
        - The credential key pair MUST be generated on the device that will use it
        - The attestation MUST be signed by the identity private key
        - The attestation MUST name credential public key, hub, subject and expiry
        - The hub MUST verify the attestation against the identity public key it
          holds from the invitation, and MUST reject any other key
        - The hub MUST store the credential public key and MUST NOT require the
          identity key again
        - A subject MAY hold several credentials at one hub, one per device
        - A passkey credential MUST be registered with the hub as the relying
          party, so the authenticator itself confines it to that hub
        - A passkey's public key MUST be attested by the identity key like any
          other credential — WebAuthn registration alone does not bind it to a
          subject, it only proves an authenticator was present
        - A credential MUST record whether it is device-bound or syncable, taken
          from the authenticator's backup-eligibility signal and never from the
          client's assertion
        - A passkey MUST NOT be used to sign an attestation or any record that
          outlives the session
        - The attestation signature MUST be ML-DSA-65. A hub MUST reject any
          other algorithm rather than verifying under whatever the attestation
          declares
        - The credential's algorithm MUST be fixed at registration and stored by
          the hub. At authentication the hub MUST verify under the STORED
          algorithm and MUST ignore any algorithm named in the assertion
        - A credential public key that has ever been revoked at this hub MUST NOT
          be re-registered, even under a valid attestation. Otherwise replaying
          the original attestation resurrects a revoked credential
        - A credential public key a hub holds, or ever held, for one subject, or
          that is any subject's identity key, MUST be refused for every other
          subject with credential_key_bound, and logged as a security event.
          A key the hub already records names that credential, so accepting
          it for a second subject would rebind the credential, and every token
          it mints, to the subject who attested it second
        - A hub MAY require the identity's signature over a fresh,
          replay-protected request before it accepts an attestation. What that
          signature obtains MUST be limited to submitting attestations for that
          identity. It is part of attesting, never a login

    - name: authentication
      description: Prove the credential at a hub
      required: true
      rules:
        - The challenge nonce MUST be issued by the hub, single-use, and bound to
          the credential. A client-chosen nonce is not a challenge
        - The hub MUST verify the challenge signature against the stored credential
          public key, under the stored algorithm
        - The hub MUST NOT accept the IDENTITY key as an authentication credential.
          The identity key attests; it never logs in. Accepting it would put the
          subject's most valuable key on the wire at every login
        - The hub MUST reject a credential that is revoked, expired, or whose
          attesting identity is revoked
        - The hub MUST apply any additional assurance its policy requires
        - Where policy requires user verification, the hub MUST verify the UV flag
          in the authenticator response and MUST NOT infer it from the presence
          of a signature
        - Where policy requires device-bound credentials, a syncable passkey MUST
          be refused at registration, not at authentication
        - On success the hub issues a WLT bounded by the grant, never wider

    - name: revocation
      description: Withdraw a credential, or an identity
      required: true
      rules:
        - Revoking a credential MUST NOT affect the identity or other credentials
        - A SUSPENDED grant MUST deny exactly as a revoked one does. Suspension
          preserves the record for later restoration; it MUST NOT confer reduced
          or read-only access as a side effect
        - Restoring a suspended grant, or widening any grant, MUST require the same
          authority as issuing it, and MUST be audited
        - Grant, credential and identity lifecycle records MUST be written to a
          tamper-evident log. A revocation that can be quietly deleted is not a
          revocation
        - Revoking an identity MUST void every credential attested by it
        - A hub MUST check credential status when a token is USED against its own
          resources, and MUST refuse a token whose credential is revoked. A WLT
          is self-contained and cannot be recalled once issued; the issuing hub
          holds the credential record, so it is the one party that can enforce
          revocation without waiting for expiry
        - Attestation `not_after` bounds the exposure where status cannot be
          checked. Implementations MUST cap attestation lifetime and SHOULD keep
          it short — it is the revocation window for every verifier that is not
          the issuing hub
        - A hub MUST refuse a request to revoke the last credential holding
          grant:issue. Rotating that subject's identity is not such a request.
          It voids the credential as identity-rotation requires, and the new
          identity attests a new one, so the hub still has a subject able to
          administer it
        - Every revocation MUST record actor, timestamp and reason

    - name: identity-rotation
      description: Replace an identity, and renegotiate everything bound to it
      required: true
      rules:
        - The rotation announcement MUST be signed by the current identity key
        - Every credential attested by the old identity MUST become invalid
        - Each hub MUST require a fresh attestation under the new identity
        - There MUST be no overlap period in which both identity keys verify
        - A hub that cannot reach the subject MUST fail closed, leaving the old
          credentials invalid rather than trusting an unverified replacement
        - A hub that verifies the announcement MUST keep the subject's grant and
          the identifier it records for the subject, and MUST record the old
          key as retired and never accept it again. Every record the hub made
          before the rotation and every record it makes after it then name the
          same subject, so a subject cannot become someone else to its own
          records by rotating
        - A subject without the old key cannot announce a rotation. It is
          admitted afresh, as a new subject with a new identifier
        - A component registered with an orchestrator is not yet under the
          rule that keeps its identifier. Its identity id is the fingerprint
          of its current key, and changes when POST /v1/rotate-key replaces
          that key. protocol/spec states that gap

    - name: service-subject-constraints
      description: What a non-human subject may not do, and must carry
      required: true
      rules:
        - A service identity MUST record a responsible human owner, by identity id
        - A service identity MUST NOT issue an invitation, vouch for a subject, or
          create another identity of any kind
        - A service credential MUST carry an expiry, and MUST be refused without one
        - A service credential SHOULD be minted per workload rather than shared, so
          revoking one running thing does not stop the others
        - A grant whose policy requires user verification MUST be refused to a
          service subject at issue, not discovered at first authentication
        - A service's operating credential MUST be short-lived, minted at process
          start, held in memory only, and never written to disk
        - A production hub MUST refuse a grant to a subject whose key_protection
          is development-only
        - No private key (an identity's or a credential's), other key material or
          passphrase MAY be passed through an environment variable
          (protocol/identity rule 9, which states the one exception)
        - In-memory key material MUST be zeroised after use and excluded from core
          dumps
        - Where the runtime can attest the workload, a service SHOULD hold no
          long-lived private key at all
        - Enrolment MUST be vouched by a subject holding grant:issue, and the
          voucher MUST be recorded on the grant
        - A service subject MUST NOT be granted a capability protocol/types
          marks person only, by any role. The hub refuses it at issue with
          capability_person_only
        - A token minted for a service subject MUST NOT carry a person-only
          capability, whatever its roles have come to hold since the grant was
          issued. The hub refuses to mint it, with capability_person_only, and
          never strips the capability and mints the rest
        - A component that registers with an orchestrator is a service subject.
          A registration whose manifest declares a person-only capability is
          refused with capability_person_only (protocol/spec, POST /v1/register).
          The enrolment rules above are not yet applied to a registration, and
          protocol/spec states that gap
        - Offboarding a human owner MUST surface every service identity they own
          rather than silently orphaning it

    - name: agent-delegation
      description: Bound what an agent may do on a subject's behalf
      required: true
      rules:
        - An agent MUST act under a subject's credential and MUST NOT hold one
        - Effective authority MUST be the intersection of the subject's grant,
          the agent's declared capabilities, and the subject's authorisation of
          that specific agent
        - Authorising a credential MUST NOT authorise every agent to use it —
          consent is per agent and per capability
        - An authorisation MUST carry an expiry and MUST be revocable by the
          subject without revoking the credential
        - An agent MUST NOT grant, delegate or pass on any capability, including
          to another agent
        - Every action MUST be attributed to both the subject and the agent run
        - Revoking the subject's grant MUST immediately end every agent acting
          under it
        - An agent acting for a PERSON MUST NOT exceed what that person could do
          directly at the same hub, in the same scope, on the same resource
        - An authorisation MUST NOT name a capability protocol/types marks person
          only. The hub refuses it with capability_person_only, so a subject's
          person-only capability never reaches an agent acting under it

    - name: cross-hub-isolation
      description: Confine a credential to the hub it was minted for
      required: true
      rules:
        - A credential MUST NOT authenticate at any hub other than the one named
          in its attestation
        - A token issued by one hub MUST NOT be accepted by another
        - Enumerating a subject's hubs MUST be answered by a directory, never by
          asking hubs to disclose their grants

    - name: record-signing
      description: A credential that signs records meant to outlive the
        session, and how a verifier that is not the hub checks one
      required: false
      rules:
        - An attestation's purpose is authenticate, the default when the field
          is absent, or sign-records. An authenticate credential proves a live
          session at the hub it names. A sign-records credential signs records
          for that hub
        - A sign-records credential MUST be an ML-DSA-65 key file. Design
          Principle 6 lets a classical key prove a session and nothing more. A
          passkey or any classical key attested for sign-records is refused with
          algorithm_not_permitted
        - A sign-records credential MUST NOT authenticate. A hub records it, with
          the lifetime cap every attestation it records has, so that it can be
          revoked and counted as any credential can. It never issues a
          challenge for one or mints a token with one, and refuses a request
          that tries with credential_purpose_mismatch. It never counts as a
          credential able to administer the hub (last_administrator)
        - One key serves one purpose. A hub MUST refuse to record a key under
          one purpose while it holds that key, or ever held it, under the
          other, with credential_purpose_mismatch
        - A hub MUST name, in every token it mints through one of a subject's
          authenticate credentials (protocol/identity, record_keys - at
          architecture/admin, every operator token), each sign-records
          credential that subject holds there that is active and unexpired. Revoking one leaves it out
          of every token minted afterwards
        - An authenticate credential MUST NOT sign a record. A verifier refuses
          a record signature made by one with credential_purpose_mismatch
        - The key a client holds in order to sign in never signs a record, and
          two parties enforce it. The hub records a key under one purpose only.
          A verifier that receives a record signature with a token refuses it,
          with credential_purpose_mismatch, unless the signing key is one of
          the token's record_keys and is not the credential the token's cid
          names. A verifier that holds no token, such as an auditor reading a
          log, can check neither, and has them only as the verifier that held
          the token recorded them
        - A sign-records credential MUST be generated on the device where its
          subject signs, and MUST NOT be generated or held by a party that acts
          for people who sign in to it
        - A verifier of a signed record is not the issuing hub, so it checks the
          attestation itself - its signature under identity_public_key; that
          identity_public_key is a key the issuing hub records for the identity
          id the record names, which is the key whose fingerprint that id is or
          one the hub accepted in its place by an identity-rotation; its
          purpose, and its hub_id against the hub the record belongs to where
          the verifier knows it - and treats not_after as the revocation window
          it has, as revocation states
        - Revocation and rotation are prospective. A signature a log recorded
          while its attestation was live stays as it was recorded, and the
          log's order says what came before and what came after. Nothing
          un-signs a record. A verifier refuses a signature presented after its
          attestation's not_after. A revoked sign-records credential, and a
          revoked or rotated identity, reach a verifier that is not the hub only
          through the hub's tokens. The credential is missing from the
          record_keys of every token minted after its revocation, and the hub
          refuses that identity's tokens. Neither reaches such a verifier sooner
          than the tokens minted before it expire

  guarantees:
    - No platform ever holds a subject's identity private key
    - A hub can authenticate its subjects with no directory reachable
    - Losing one device costs one credential
    - Losing an identity costs every credential, and nothing less
    - A hub always retains at least one subject able to administer it
```

## Types

```yaml
types:
  Identity:
    description: A subject's durable identity. Generated and held by the subject
    fields:
      id:
        type: string
        required: true
        description: Stable identifier. Never reused, never derived from a name
      public_key:
        type: string
        required: true
        description: ML-DSA-65 public key (base64url). The identity itself
      kind:
        type: enum
        values: [user, service]
        required: true
        description: Operational only. Does not change the model, but a service
          is constrained by Design Principle 11
      owner_identity_id:
        type: string
        required: false
        description: REQUIRED when kind is service. The human answerable for it.
          Offboarding that human MUST surface this identity, not orphan it
      key_protection:
        type: enum
        values: [hardware, platform-attestation, secrets-manager, passphrase,
                 development-only]
        required: true
        description: How the long-lived key is protected. `development-only` is
          file permissions alone and MUST NOT be accepted by a production hub —
          see protocol/identity Service Key Protection. protocol/secret-source
          says which value each way of holding the key earns
      display_name:
        type: string
        required: false
        description: For humans. MAY change. MUST NOT be used as an identifier
      status:
        type: enum
        values: [active, revoked]
        required: true
      created_at:
        type: timestamp
        required: true

  Attestation:
    description: A signed statement binding a credential to an identity
    fields:
      identity_id:
        type: string
        required: true
        description: The identifier the hub records for the subject, which it
          keeps across an identity-rotation it verified. At architecture/admin,
          the operator's identity id
      identity_public_key:
        type: string
        required: true
        description: The key the signature is verified against
      credential_public_key:
        type: string
        required: true
      hub_id:
        type: string
        required: true
        description: The ONE hub this credential may authenticate at, or, for a
          sign-records credential, the one hub whose records it signs. Named by
          the fingerprint (protocol/identity) of the first key that hub signed
          its tokens with, which the hub records and keeps when it rotates that
          key — never by its address. A hub that moves or rotates its key is the
          same hub, and a hub regenerated without its records is a different one
      purpose:
        type: enum
        values: [authenticate, sign-records]
        required: false
        description: What the credential is for. Absent means authenticate. A
          sign-records credential never authenticates, and an authenticate
          credential never signs a record (record-signing)
      device_label:
        type: string
        required: false
        description: For humans, so a revocation list is legible
      not_after:
        type: timestamp
        required: true
      signature:
        type: string
        required: true
        description: ML-DSA-65 signature by the identity private key over the
          RFC 8785 canonical form of every other field present, as
          protocol/spec signs every JSON payload

  Credential:
    description: What a hub stores and authenticates against. A credential is
      named by the fingerprint (protocol/identity) of its credential_public_key,
      and a token minted with it carries that name as `cid`
    fields:
      credential_public_key:
        type: string
        required: true
      algorithm:
        type: enum
        values: [ml-dsa-65, es256, eddsa]
        required: true
        description: Declared, never assumed. Classical algorithms are permitted
          here and nowhere else in this pattern
      form:
        type: enum
        values: [key-file, passkey]
        required: true
      device_bound:
        type: boolean
        required: true
        description: False for a syncable passkey, which may exist on several
          devices at once. Taken from the authenticator, not from the client
      authenticator:
        type: string
        required: false
        description: Authenticator model identifier (AAGUID) where attested.
          Absent means the authenticator did not attest its provenance
      identity_id:
        type: string
        required: true
      attestation:
        type: Attestation
        required: true
      status:
        type: enum
        values: [active, revoked, expired]
        required: true
      last_used_at:
        type: timestamp
        required: false

  Invitation:
    description: An offer of admission, bound to a known identity key
    fields:
      invitation_id:
        type: string
        required: true
      identity_public_key:
        type: string
        required: true
        description: Obtained out-of-band BEFORE issue. The binding anchor
      key_provenance:
        type: enum
        values: [verified-in-person, verified-fingerprint, vouched, unverified]
        required: true
        description: How the inviter obtained the key. Recorded, not assumed
      offered_roles:
        type: list
        required: true
      offered_scope:
        type: ScopeLevel
        required: true
      expires_at:
        type: timestamp
        required: true
      status:
        type: enum
        values: [pending, redeemed, declined, revoked, expired]
        required: true

  Grant:
    description: What a subject may do at this hub. Outlives any one credential
    fields:
      identity_id:
        type: string
        required: true
      roles:
        type: list
        required: true
      scope:
        type: ScopeLevel
        required: true
      assurance:
        type: AssurancePolicy
        required: true
      issued_by:
        type: string
        required: true
        description: identity_id of the issuer, or "bootstrap" for the first
      issued_at:
        type: timestamp
        required: true
      status:
        type: enum
        values: [active, suspended, revoked]
        required: true

  AgentAuthorization:
    description: A subject's consent for one agent to act in their name
    fields:
      subject_identity_id:
        type: string
        required: true
      agent_id:
        type: string
        required: true
        description: One agent. An authorisation MUST NOT name a set or a wildcard
      capabilities:
        type: list
        required: true
        description: A subset of what the subject's grant permits. MUST NOT exceed it
      resources:
        type: list
        required: false
        description: Narrowing to named resources — this calendar, that folder
      not_after:
        type: timestamp
        required: true
      granted_at:
        type: timestamp
        required: true
      revoked_at:
        type: timestamp
        required: false

  AssurancePolicy:
    description: What this hub demands beyond a valid credential signature
    fields:
      minimum_level:
        type: enum
        values: [aal1, aal2, aal3]
        required: true
      session_ttl:
        type: duration
        required: true
      require_user_verification:
        type: boolean
        required: false
        description: Demand a biometric or PIN check at authentication. This is

      minimum_key_provenance:
        type: enum
        values: [verified-in-person, verified-fingerprint, vouched, unverified]
        required: false
        description: Refuse to issue a grant at or above a given scope unless the

      require_device_bound:
        type: boolean
        required: false
        description: Refuse syncable credentials. A synced passkey may exist on

      accepted_authenticators:
        type: list
        required: false
        description: AAGUIDs permitted where a hub requires certified hardware.
          Empty means any authenticator is acceptable
      additional_checks:
        type: list
        required: false
        description: External checks that MAY deny or raise assurance — device
          compliance, MFA, employment status. An external provider MUST NOT be
          able to ASSERT identity; it may only refuse or strengthen
```

| Code | Category | When | Behaviour |
|------|----------|------|-----------|
| `identity_unknown` | auth | No credential maps to this identity here | Deny. MUST NOT reveal whether it exists elsewhere |
| `identity_revoked` | auth | The attesting identity is revoked | Deny, void every credential it attested |
| `identity_mismatch` | auth | Presented key differs from the invitation's | Refuse redemption, require reissue |
| `credential_revoked` | auth | This device's credential was withdrawn | Deny, leave identity and siblings intact |
| `credential_foreign_hub` | auth | Attestation names a different hub | Deny. Log as a security event |
| `attestation_invalid` | auth | Signature does not verify | Refuse. Log as a security event |
| `attestation_expired` | auth | `not_after` has passed | Deny, require re-attestation |
| `invitation_spent` | validation | Already redeemed, declined or revoked | Refuse |
| `invitation_expired` | validation | Past `expires_at` | Refuse, require reissue |
| `replay_detected` | auth | Nonce reused or outside the 300s window | Deny. Log as a security event |
| `no_subject` | config | Hub creation attempted with no identity | Refuse creation |
| `last_administrator` | config | Revoking the last `grant:issue` holder | Refuse |
| `assurance_unmet` | auth | Policy not satisfied | Deny, naming the unmet requirement |
| `user_verification_absent` | auth | Policy requires UV; the UV flag is unset | Deny. MUST NOT infer UV from a valid signature |
| `credential_syncable_refused` | validation | Policy requires device-bound; the passkey is syncable | Refuse at REGISTRATION, so the subject learns before relying on it |
| `authenticator_untrusted` | validation | AAGUID outside `accepted_authenticators` | Refuse registration |
| `algorithm_not_permitted` | validation | A classical key offered as an identity, to sign an attestation, or as a sign-records credential | Refuse. Log as a security event |
| `credential_purpose_mismatch` | auth | A credential used for what its attestation's `purpose` excludes: a sign-records credential offered to authenticate; a key offered under one purpose that the hub holds, or held, under the other; an authenticate credential's signature offered on a record; or a record signature, arriving with a token, by a key that is not one of the token's `record_keys` or is the credential its `cid` names | Refuse. Log as a security event |
| `credential_previously_revoked` | validation | Re-registering a key revoked here before | Refuse permanently. Log as a security event |
| `credential_key_bound` | validation | A credential key the hub holds, or held, for another subject, or that is a subject's identity key, attested for a second subject | Refuse permanently. Log as a security event |
| `identity_key_as_credential` | auth | The identity key was offered to authenticate | Deny. Log as a security event |
| `scope_exceeds_issuer` | validation | Invitation offers more than the issuer holds | Refuse |
| `provenance_insufficient` | validation | Key provenance below `minimum_key_provenance` for the offered scope | Refuse |
| `token_credential_revoked` | auth | Valid signature, revoked credential | Deny, without waiting for expiry |
| `service_cannot_delegate` | validation | A service tried to invite, vouch, or create an identity | Refuse. Log as a security event |
| `agent_unauthorised` | auth | No authorisation for this agent under this subject | Deny. An agent is not covered by its subject's grant alone |
| `agent_exceeds_subject` | auth | Agent asked for more than the subject may do | Deny. Log as a security event |
| `agent_authorisation_expired` | auth | Past `not_after` | Deny, without extending |
| `agent_cannot_delegate` | validation | An agent tried to pass on a capability | Refuse. Log as a security event |
| `service_owner_required` | validation | Service identity with no human owner | Refuse enrolment |
| `credential_expiry_required` | validation | Service credential offered with no expiry | Refuse |
| `key_protection_insufficient` | validation | Scope exceeds what this key protection permits | Refuse, naming the required protection |
| `development_key_in_production` | validation | `development-only` protection at a production hub | Refuse outright. Log as a security event |
| `credential_persisted` | integrity | A service credential was found written to disk | Refuse and revoke it — it must be memory-only |
| `presence_unsatisfiable` | validation | Grant requires user verification; subject is a service | Refuse at ISSUE, not at first login |
| `capability_person_only` | validation | A grant to a service subject, a token minted for one, a registration manifest, or an agent's authorisation carries a capability `protocol/types` marks person only | Refuse at ISSUE. Log as a security event |
| `clock_outside_tolerance` | auth | Timestamp beyond skew tolerance, or not yet valid | Deny. MUST NOT clamp into range |

These codes name conditions, and a surface that raises one gives it the wire
form its own blueprint registers, as `architecture/admin`'s Error Handling does
for the operator routes. `capability_person_only` is raised on several
surfaces, so its wire form is registered once, in
[`protocol/types`](../protocol/types.md#error-handling): `CAPABILITY_PERSON_ONLY`,
HTTP 403, permanent.

Every failure is fail-closed. An unreachable directory causes discovery to fail;
it MUST NOT cause a hub to accept unverified key material.

## Implementation Notes

**Nothing but the subject generates an identity.** The most damaging way to
implement this pattern is a friendly "create an account for Alice" button that
mints her key pair server-side. The platform is then a key escrow for every
identity it created, and the whole model collapses to trusting that platform. If
a control plane offers identity creation, the key pair MUST be generated
client-side in the browser with the Web Crypto API, or by the CLI on the
subject's own machine, and the private half MUST NOT be transmitted.

**The trust anchor is how the inviter got the public key.** Binding the invite
to a known key defeats interception — an attacker holding the invitation cannot
redeem it without the private key. But if the attacker supplied the key in the
first place, every later control works perfectly for the wrong subject. That
step is out-of-band by necessity, which is why `key_provenance` is recorded
rather than assumed: an auditor can then separate grants resting on a verified
binding from grants resting on a pasted string.

**Bootstrap is trust-on-first-use, and "first to arrive" is a land grab.** The
first identity to create a hub is verified by nobody, because there is nobody to
verify against — but an unauthenticated bootstrap endpoint reachable on a network
is claimable by whoever reaches it first, which need not be the operator who
started the process. Implementations MUST require a bootstrap secret emitted at
first start where only whoever started the process can read it — a file only
that user can read, or a terminal attached to the process — and never to a log,
which others read and which outlives the claim. They MUST NOT treat mere arrival
as authority.
Implementations SHOULD additionally confine bootstrap to console or local access,
time-box it, close the window permanently once claimed, and record the resulting
grant with `key_provenance: unverified`.
Implementations SHOULD also support transferring the bootstrap grant to the
tenant's own administrator and removing the creator's — that transfer is what
distinguishes provisioning a hub for someone from retaining a way into it.

**Keep the identity key cold.** Once credentials are minted, the identity
private key is needed only to attest new devices or new hubs. It SHOULD live on
removable or hardware-backed storage, and implementations SHOULD NOT cache it or
prompt for it during ordinary work. Its rarity of use is most of its protection.

**Possession is not presence.** A stolen unlocked device authenticates. The
passphrase requirement helps, hardware-backed credential storage helps more, and
`additional_checks` lets a hub demand device compliance or a second factor. Note
the direction of that arrow: an external provider may DENY or RAISE assurance
and may never ASSERT identity. Reversing it makes that provider a skeleton key
for every hub that trusts it.

**Passkeys are the credential layer done in hardware — with three catches.**
A WebAuthn credential is already per-relying-party, generated on the device and
non-exportable, so the browser itself refuses to offer a hub-A passkey to hub B.
That is stronger isolation than a check in application code. The catches:
(1) passkeys sign with ES256 or EdDSA, so they authenticate and never attest —
the identity key still signs the binding; (2) a SYNCED passkey lives on every
device its provider has enrolled, which quietly returns per-device revocation to
per-account revocation, so a hub wanting the former must set
`require_device_bound` and refuse at registration; (3) user verification is the
one signal in this whole model that speaks to a human being present rather than
a key being possessed, and it must be read from the authenticator's UV flag
rather than inferred.

**Recovery from a lost authenticator runs through the identity.** A subject whose
only passkey was on a lost phone is locked out of that hub, and the fix is to
attest a new credential with the identity key — not to weaken the hub. This is
the strongest practical argument for keeping the identity key backed up and cold:
it is the recovery path for every credential a subject holds anywhere.

**A service key on disk is a permanent, silent compromise.** `protocol/identity`
permits `kdf: none` in development only, where no passphrase can be prompted,
protected only by file permissions. Anyone who reads that file — from a backup,
a container image, a mounted volume, a compromised sidecar — holds that identity
indefinitely, and nothing about the theft is observable: there is no second
factor to fail and no presence check to miss. The fix is not stronger encryption
of that file — it is to stop having one. A service's operating credential should
be minted at process start, live in memory for hours, and never touch disk; the
long-lived material that attests it belongs in hardware, a KMS, or nowhere at
all where the platform can attest the workload directly. Mint one credential per
workload, so a single compromised container carries its own authority and
nothing else. `protocol/identity` Service Key Protection sets the conformance
bar, and `development-only` protection is exactly that.

**Service accounts drift, because nobody notices them.** A person reports a lost
laptop within the hour. A service key sits for years, outliving the project, the
team and often the person who created it. Two habits close most of this: bind
every service identity to a named human owner and surface those identities when
that person is offboarded; and give service credentials a mandatory expiry so
neglect ends in an outage rather than in a standing key nobody remembers.
`protocol/identity` recommends 90-day rotation — for service credentials, treat
that as the ceiling rather than the target.

**Nothing that cannot judge may delegate.** A service must not invite, vouch, or
create identities. This is not a policy preference: an agent running under a
service credential is steered by model output over untrusted content, so a
service that can mint service accounts turns one prompt injection into permanent
independent access. Every boundary in the system would then be exactly one call
deep.

**Service accounts need a voucher.** A service cannot verify a fingerprint over
the phone. Its invitation MUST be issued by an operator who vouches for the key,
and the grant records that operator. Where a service's credential is used by an
agent, the agent's authority is the intersection of that grant and the agent's
own declaration — the credential does not widen it.

**Rotation is a project, not a click.** Rotating an identity invalidates every
attestation it signed, everywhere. Implementations SHOULD show the subject which
hubs will be affected before proceeding, and SHOULD NOT offer identity rotation
as a routine convenience. When a single device is lost, the correct operation is
credential revocation, which costs nothing else.

## Verification Checklist

1. Identity generation never transmits a private key — a network capture during
   enrolment contains only the public half.
2. A hub with no identity cannot be created; creation is refused with `no_subject`.
3. An invitation naming Alice's key cannot be redeemed by any other key —
   redemption with a different key fails with `identity_mismatch`.
4. An intercepted invitation is useless without the corresponding private key.
5. A redeemed invitation cannot be redeemed twice — the second attempt fails with
   `invitation_spent`.
6. A credential attested for hub A is rejected at hub B with
   `credential_foreign_hub`.
7. Revoking one device's credential leaves the subject able to authenticate from
   another device at the same hub.
8. Revoking an identity invalidates every credential attested by it, at every hub
   holding one.
9. After identity rotation, no credential attested by the previous key
   authenticates anywhere — there is no window in which both verify.
10. A hub authenticates its subjects with every directory unreachable.
11. Replacing a subject's key in a directory does not change who can authenticate
    at any hub.
12. `key_provenance` is present on every grant, and grants created at bootstrap
    record `unverified`.
13. An external assurance check can deny a login, and cannot cause one to succeed
    for an identity whose credential signature failed.
14. A service identity follows the same enrolment, attestation and revocation
    paths as a human one, differing only in `kdf`.
15. A passkey registered at hub A is never offered by the browser at hub B, and is
    rejected if replayed there.
16. A passkey is refused when offered as an identity or to sign an attestation,
    with `algorithm_not_permitted`.
17. Under `require_device_bound`, a syncable passkey is refused at registration —
    not accepted and then rejected at first login.
18. Under `require_user_verification`, an assertion with a valid signature and the
    UV flag unset is denied with `user_verification_absent`.
19. A subject who loses every authenticator at a hub recovers by attesting a new
    credential with the identity key, and by no other route.
20. Replaying a valid attestation for a revoked credential does not resurrect it —
    it fails with `credential_previously_revoked`.
21. An assertion naming a different algorithm than the one stored at registration
    is verified under the STORED algorithm, and fails.
22. Offering the identity key as an authentication credential is denied with
    `identity_key_as_credential`.
23. An invitation offering roles the issuer does not hold is refused with
    `scope_exceeds_issuer`.
24. Roles or scope supplied at redemption are ignored — the resulting grant matches
    the invitation exactly.
25. A challenge nonce cannot be reused, and a client-supplied nonce is refused.
26. A token bearing a valid signature for a revoked credential is refused at the
    issuing hub without waiting for expiry.
27. Bootstrap cannot be claimed by reaching the endpoint alone — it requires the
    secret emitted at first start, and the window closes permanently once claimed.
28. A service subject attempting to issue an invitation, vouch, or create an
    identity is refused with `service_cannot_delegate`.
29. Enrolling a service identity with no human owner is refused; offboarding that
    human lists every service identity they own.
30. A service credential offered without an expiry is refused.
31. A grant requiring user verification is refused to a service subject at ISSUE,
    with `presence_unsatisfiable` — never accepted and then unusable.
32. A key protected only by file permissions is refused by a production hub with
    `development_key_in_production`, at any scope.
33. A service's operating credential never appears on disk — a filesystem scan
    during and after a run finds no credential material.
34. A private key (an identity's or a credential's), other key material or a
    passphrase supplied through an environment variable is refused
    (`protocol/identity` rule 9, which states the one exception).
35. A suspended grant denies exactly as a revoked one does — it confers no
    read-only residue.
36. Restoring or widening a grant requires the same authority as issuing it, and
    appears in the tamper-evident log.
37. An attestation whose validity begins in the future is rejected, and a
    timestamp beyond skew tolerance is rejected rather than clamped.
38. An agent with no authorisation is denied even when its subject's grant would
    permit the action, with `agent_unauthorised`.
39. Authorising agent A does not permit agent B to act under the same credential.
40. An agent asking for a capability its subject lacks is denied with
    `agent_exceeds_subject` — the intersection never widens.
41. Revoking the subject's grant immediately stops every agent acting under it.
42. An agent attempting to pass a capability to another agent is refused.
43. Every agent action names both the subject and the agent run in the record.
44. Whatever a hub issues on the strength of an identity signature submits an
    attestation for that identity and is refused for every other request.
45. A grant to a service subject carrying any person-only capability —
    `content:write`, `content:decide`, `content:replicate`, `content:connect`,
    `content:declassify`, `model:attest` or `fabric:attest` — is refused at
    issue with `capability_person_only`.
46. An `AgentAuthorization` naming any of those seven is refused with
    `capability_person_only`, so an agent acting under a subject who holds
    `content:write` cannot write an entry directly.
47. A service subject whose role comes to carry any of those seven after its
    grant was issued is refused a token with `capability_person_only`, and is
    never issued one with the capability removed.
48. A registration whose manifest declares any of those seven is refused with
    `capability_person_only`, whose wire form is `CAPABILITY_PERSON_ONLY`, and
    no token is issued.
49. An attestation with `purpose` `sign-records` is recorded, can be revoked,
    and never obtains a challenge or a token: a request for either is refused
    with `credential_purpose_mismatch`.
50. An attestation with `purpose` `sign-records` for a passkey or any classical
    key is refused with `algorithm_not_permitted`.
51. A record signature made by a credential whose attestation has no `purpose`,
    or `purpose` `authenticate`, is refused with `credential_purpose_mismatch`.
52. A signature a log recorded while its attestation was live still verifies
    after the attestation's `not_after` has passed, and the same signature
    presented after that is refused.
53. A key the hub holds under one purpose is refused under the other with
    `credential_purpose_mismatch`, and so is a key it held under the other
    and revoked.
54. A token minted through a subject's authenticate credential names in `record_keys` each of that subject's
    active, unexpired sign-records credentials at the hub, and none revoked
    before the token was minted.
55. A record signature that arrives with a token is refused with
    `credential_purpose_mismatch` where its key is not one of the token's
    `record_keys`, or is the credential the token's `cid` names.
56. Revoking the last active authenticate credential of the last subject able
    to administer a hub is refused with `last_administrator`, however many
    sign-records credentials that subject holds.
57. A subject attesting a credential key the hub holds, or held, for another
    subject, or another subject's identity key, is refused with
    `credential_key_bound`; the other subject's credential keeps its subject,
    and the next token it mints is that subject's.
58. After a rotation the hub verified, the subject's next token carries the
    same identifier as before it, and an attestation by the new identity key
    names that identifier; the old key is refused as an identity key and as a
    credential.
