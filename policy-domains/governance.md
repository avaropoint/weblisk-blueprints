requires: [policy, approval]
boundaries:
  compliance_audit: >-
    Setting direction: structure, roles, authority, oversight, what the organisation commits to. Checking whether it happened is compliance_audit's.
  risk_management: >-
    Who decides, on what authority, and how the organisation is directed. Identifying and sizing what could go wrong is risk_management's.
---

## What this covers

How the organisation is directed and who answers for what: structure, positions, delegated authority, oversight bodies, terms of reference, and the commitments the organisation makes about its own conduct. It is the domain that says who is allowed to decide, and it is therefore the one every other domain leans on when it names a position.

## Why these requirements

A policy, because governance that is not written down is a habit, and a habit cannot be delegated, disputed or inherited. An approval, because a governing document nobody put in force is a draft with a confident tone — the signature is the whole difference between a commitment and an intention. Nothing else is required here: governance produces very little standing work of its own, and demanding a register of it would manufacture a filing task to satisfy a rule.

## How it goes wrong

It becomes a folder of org charts. The test is whether anything else in the programme can point at a decision made here; if every other domain names positions this domain never establishes, governance is being claimed rather than done.
