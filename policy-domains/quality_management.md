## What this covers

Whether the work meets the specification the organisation set for it: requirements, inspection and test, nonconformance, corrective action, and continual improvement of the process that produced the work.

## Why these requirements

An approval, because a nonconformance disposition — use as is, rework, reject — is a decision with a signature behind it, and an unsigned one is an opinion. A register of nonconformances, because the pattern across them is the improvement. A policy, because the quality commitment is what the specification is measured against.

## How it goes wrong

Corrective action treats the instance and not the cause, so the register grows and the same nonconformance recurs under new numbers.
