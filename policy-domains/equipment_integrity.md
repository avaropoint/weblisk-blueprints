## What this covers

Whether a piece of equipment is fit to be in service: inspection, testing, certification, maintenance, defect reporting, and taking it out of service when it is not.

## Why these requirements

Triggered work, because inspection intervals run off each item's own last-inspected date, not off a shared calendar — a fleet on a single annual sweep has most of its items inspected at the wrong time. A register, because the interval is per item and has to be held per item. Retention, because the certificate history is what establishes fitness at a past date, which is the question asked after a failure.

## How it goes wrong

Out-of-service is a sticker rather than a state. If the register cannot express that an item is withdrawn, it will keep being scheduled and keep appearing compliant.
