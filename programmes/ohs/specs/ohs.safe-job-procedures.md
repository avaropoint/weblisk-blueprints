What this document must establish for THIS organisation: how a safe job procedure
is written, approved and kept current — and which jobs must have one.

It is a procedure ABOUT procedures, and the distinction matters: the individual
job procedures are content this organisation authors per task, and what belongs
here is the method that makes them consistent and the rule that decides which
tasks need one.

It must state the trigger. A task earns a safe job procedure when its hazard
assessment finds a risk that administrative control alone will not hold — the
critical tasks, not every task. An organisation that writes one for everything
produces a library nobody opens, and the standard is not asking for volume.

It must require each procedure to be written as an ORDERED SEQUENCE, because that
is what separates it from a practice: the order is the control. A step performed
out of sequence in an isolation procedure is the incident.

It must require the hierarchy of controls to be applied and the choice recorded,
so that a procedure relying on personal protective equipment shows why
elimination, substitution and engineering were rejected. Recording the choice is
what makes a control chosen for convenience visible as such — and it is the same
requirement the hazard assessment procedure makes, deliberately, because the two
must not disagree.

It must say who writes one, who reviews it, who approves it, and that the people
who do the work were involved. A procedure written entirely in an office
describes the job somebody imagines.
