What this document must establish for THIS organisation: the general way each
kind of hazardous work is done safely, and the rules that apply to everybody
regardless of the task.

The two halves are different things and the document must keep them apart. A
PRACTICE is guidance for a type of work — working at height, confined space,
hot work, lifting, traffic control — and it explains how to think about the
hazard. A RULE is a condition of employment: it is not advice, breaking it has a
consequence, and the consequence must be stated. A document that blends them
produces rules nobody enforces and guidance people are disciplined for.

Practices must be named per KIND OF WORK this organisation actually performs, and
that list comes from the hazard assessments rather than from a template. A
practice for work the organisation never does is a document to maintain for no
reason; a kind of work with no practice is the gap this artifact exists to expose.

Each practice must say what the hazard is, what the controls are in the order the
hierarchy requires — elimination, substitution, engineering, administration,
personal protective equipment — and who may authorise the work. Where a practice
is task-specific enough to be a sequence of steps, it belongs in a safe job
procedure instead, and this document should say which.

Rules must be few enough to be remembered and specific enough to be applied.
"Work safely" is not a rule; "nobody works alone above two metres" is.
