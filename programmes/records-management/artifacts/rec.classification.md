What this document must establish for THIS organisation: how a record is
filed so that somebody who did not create it can find it, and so that the
retention schedule can be applied to it without a person reading each one.

It must produce a structure that maps to the retention schedule's classes. A
filing scheme that cannot be joined to the schedule means every disposition is a
manual review of individual documents, which is why most schedules are never
executed — the work is not the decision, it is finding the records the decision
applies to.

It must be based on function rather than on department. Departments are
reorganised every few years and the records outlive several arrangements; a
scheme built on what the record is for survives, and one built on who happened
to own it in 2021 requires a migration nobody will do.

It must state the naming and version conventions in force, and be realistic
about enforcement. A convention nobody follows is worse than none, because the
half of the estate that follows it creates a false impression that search will
work.

It must say how it relates to the information classification standard in the
security programme, which answers a different question: that one says how
sensitive a record is and who may see it, this one says what kind of record it is
and where it lives. A document carries both, and an organisation that conflates
them ends up with a folder called "confidential" as its only classification.

It carries no obligation of its own. The recurring work is the annual programme
review and the disposition cycle; this standard is the criterion those measure
against.
