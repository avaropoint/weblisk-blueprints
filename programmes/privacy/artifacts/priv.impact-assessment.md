What this document must establish for THIS organisation: when a privacy
assessment has to be done, who does it, and what a decision to proceed depends
on.

It must name the triggers precisely, because an assessment procedure with a
vague trigger is never invoked. A new collection of personal information, a new
purpose for information already held, a new system or supplier that will hold
it, a transfer to another country, any automated processing that makes or
significantly informs a decision about a person, and any change that increases
the sensitivity or volume held — those are the six, and they belong in the
change management process as a question at approval rather than in a document
somebody has to remember.

It must be proportionate. A four-page assessment for a new mailing list and the
same four pages for a new HR system produces one that is skipped and one that is
rushed. A screening question set that ends most assessments in twenty minutes and
escalates the rest is what gets used.

It must require the decision to be recorded by somebody with the authority to
accept the residual risk, and that person must not be the person delivering the
project.

It must cover the Quebec position honestly. Law 25 requires a privacy impact
assessment for any project of acquisition, development or overhaul of an
information system or electronic service delivery involving personal
information, and for any communication of personal information outside Quebec.
PIPEDA has no equivalent statutory requirement — the Commissioner recommends
assessments and accountability requires something like them, but an organisation
with no Quebec operations that says its assessments are legally required has
mis-stated its own obligations, which is its own kind of governance failure.

It carries no obligation of its own, on purpose. An assessment is triggered by a
change and has no period; attaching a cadence would raise occurrences for work
nobody has been asked to do and would report as complete in a quarter where
nothing was assessed because nothing changed. What recurs is the annual review
that counts changes against assessments.
