# Untitled Safe Job Procedure

> [!NOTE]
> **`kind: sop`, not `kind: procedure`, and the difference is real.** An SOP
> inherits everything a procedure has and adds one capability: it is
> **agent-executable** — it carries steps, roles and required inputs in a form an
> agent may be asked to perform or to verify. A management procedure describes
> how the organisation runs something; this describes how one task is done, in
> order, by the person doing it.
>
> Write one for a **critical task** — one whose hazard assessment found a risk
> that administrative control alone will not hold. Not for every task. An
> organisation that writes one for everything produces a library nobody opens.

**Why this exists.** A safe job procedure is what separates a *practice*
(guidance for a type of work) from a *procedure* (this task, these steps, this
order). A step performed out of sequence in an isolation procedure **is** the
incident. ISO 45001:2018 cl. 8.1.2 requires the hierarchy of controls to be
applied; cl. 8.1.1 requires operational controls to be established and
implemented.

## Task

What job this covers, where it is done, and what it does **not** cover. A
procedure whose scope is vague will be applied to a job it was not written for.

## Who may do this work

The competency required, the training that evidences it, and who authorises the
work to start. Name **positions**, never people.

## Equipment and materials

Tools, equipment, permits and documents that must be present before step 1.
Include the protective equipment, and say which of it is specific to this task
rather than a site rule.

## Hazards and controls

| Hazard | Control | Highest control applied |
| --- | --- | --- |
|  |  | Eliminated / Substituted / Engineering / Administrative / PPE only |

Record **why** a control was chosen. A procedure relying on personal protective
equipment must show why elimination, substitution and engineering were rejected —
recording the choice is what makes a control chosen for convenience visible as
such.

## Steps, in order

1. **Step** — what is done, by whom, and how you know it worked before moving on.
2. **Step** —
3. **Step** —

The order is the control. If two steps can be done in either order, say so; if
they cannot, say what goes wrong.

## Stop conditions

What makes the crew stop, who is called, and how the decision is recorded either
way. A procedure with no stop-work path has already decided the answer.

## Emergency and rescue

What happens if it goes wrong **during** this task — rescue arrangement, first
aid, who is notified. A rescue plan that consists of calling 911 is a plan for a
different job.

## Verification

How somebody confirms this procedure was followed, and where that record lives.

## Review

Who wrote it, who reviewed it, who approved it, and that **the people who do the
work were involved**. A procedure written entirely in an office describes the job
somebody imagines. Review it when the task changes, after an incident involving
it, and on the interval the programme sets.
