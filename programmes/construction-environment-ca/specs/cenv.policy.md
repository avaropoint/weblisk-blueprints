What this document must establish for THIS organisation: that it will not
discharge a contaminant that causes or may cause an adverse effect, who inside
the company is answerable for that, and what a person on a site is to do when
something has gone into the ground, the air or the water that was not meant to.

It must name **one position** as the environmental lead and say what that
position may decide. The single most common failure in this area is not
ignorance of the law; it is that the decision to stop an excavation, to refuse a
load, or to telephone the Spills Action Centre sits with nobody in particular
and is therefore made late by whoever is standing there. A policy that assigns
the duty to "management" has assigned it to no one.

It must state the organisation's position on **who the project leader is**. Under
O. Reg. 406/19 the project leader is the person or persons ultimately responsible
for making decisions relating to the planning and implementation of the project.
On a stipulated-price job for a municipality that is usually the owner; on a
design-build or a self-performed development it is usually this organisation.
The policy cannot decide it once for all projects — it must require that the
question be answered and recorded for each one, and say who answers it. An
organisation that assumes it is never the project leader will discover it was
one when a Registry notice was never filed.

It must say that the organisation does not rely on a sub-trade's or a hauler's
assurance as its own compliance. The duties in O. Reg. 406/19 run against the
project leader and the operator of the project area; a contractual promise from
a soil broker transfers commercial risk and transfers no statutory duty at all.

It must state the relationship with the health and safety programme rather than
restate it. Designated substances, asbestos and worker exposure are governed by
the *Occupational Health and Safety Act* and its regulations and are answered by
the construction health and safety programme. What this policy covers is the
same material leaving the site: the contaminated soil the health and safety
survey found is an environmental obligation the moment it is loaded into a truck,
and the two programmes must not each assume the other has it.

It must be **approved by senior management and reviewed when the law changes**,
not on a comfortable annual cycle alone. This is an area where the instruments
move: the excess soil regulation has been amended in most years since it was
made, and the last tranche of its phase-in commenced on 1 January 2026.
