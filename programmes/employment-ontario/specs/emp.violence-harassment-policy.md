What this document must establish for THIS organisation: the employer's stated
position on workplace violence and on workplace harassment — two policies, in
one document or two, both signed and both posted.

It must keep the two apart, because the Act does and because the duties differ.
Workplace violence is the exercise of physical force, an attempt at it, or a
statement or behaviour a worker could reasonably interpret as a threat.
Workplace harassment is a course of vexatious comment or conduct that is known
or ought reasonably to be known to be unwelcome, and it expressly includes
workplace sexual harassment. A single paragraph covering "violence and
harassment" together has stated neither position clearly enough for a worker to
know which one they are reporting.

It must say, in the organisation's own voice, **what will not be tolerated and
what the employer undertakes to do about it** — not what the Act says. A policy
that recites s. 32.0.1 has told the workforce that the employer read the
statute, which is not the same as the employer having a position.

It must state that reporting attracts **no reprisal**, and mean it in the terms
s. 50 uses: no dismissal, discipline, penalty, coercion or intimidation because
a worker complied with the Act, sought its enforcement, or gave evidence. This
is the sentence that decides whether the reporting route in the programme is
ever used, and it belongs in the policy because it is a commitment rather than
a step.

It must name **who a worker reports to, including when that person is the
employer or the supervisor**. The Act names that case explicitly and it is the
one most often left out; a policy with a single reporting line describes an
organisation where the most serious complaints have nowhere to go.

It must say **who is accountable** — by position, never by name — for the
policies, for the programmes that implement them, and for deciding that an
investigation is complete.

It must state the scope honestly: who is covered, including workers who are not
employees, contractors on site, and clients or members of the public where the
work brings them into contact with workers. Violence from outside the workforce
is the risk in most of the sectors where this duty bites hardest.

It must say where it is **posted** and that it is **reviewed at least
annually** — s. 32.0.1(2) requires the policies to be posted in a conspicuous
place in the workplace where the employer regularly employs more than five
workers, and s. 32.0.1(1)(c) sets the review interval. Both are facts about
this document rather than about the programme, which is why they are stated
here.

**This is the position; the programme is how it is carried out.** The reporting
route, the investigation method, the risk assessment and the domestic violence
measures live in the workplace violence and harassment procedure that requires
this document. The two must not restate each other: a policy that describes an
investigation and a programme that describes a commitment will disagree the
first time either is edited.
