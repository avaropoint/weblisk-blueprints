What this document must establish for THIS organisation: that money received on
account of a contract price is not the organisation's money, that ten per cent
of every contract and subcontract under which a lien may arise is retained and
accounted for separately, and that the holdback is released on the schedule the
Construction Act sets — not when somebody remembers, and not when a project
manager is comfortable.

**It is a policy about somebody else's money.** The trust provisions make every
amount received on account of a contract price trust money in the hands of the
person who received it, to be held for the people who supplied the services and
materials it was paid for. That is the fact the rest of the programme is built
on, and it is the one most often described as an accounting convention. It is
not: a breach of trust reaches directors, officers and anybody with effective
control **personally**, and it is not discharged by the company's insolvency.
The policy must say who in this organisation is a trustee of construction trust
money, and it must name them by position.

**The holdback is a statutory obligation in both directions.** This organisation
retains ten per cent from the people it pays and has ten per cent retained by the
people who pay it. Both belong in the same account and the same register, because
the exposure is not symmetrical: holdback owed *out* is money the organisation is
holding and will have to produce, and holdback owed *in* is money it has already
earned and has not been paid. An organisation that tracks only one of them can
answer half of the question its bank and its bonding company will ask.

**Retaining is required; withholding is not the same act and must never be
described as one.** The Act requires the holdback to be *retained* until the
liens it secures have expired or been satisfied, and then requires it to be
*paid*. Refusing to pay a holdback that has fallen due is withholding, and the
Act permits that only in the circumstances and by the notices it prescribes.
The policy must state plainly that "we are not happy with the sub-trade's
paperwork" is not one of them, and must then state what this organisation
actually does in that situation — which is the subject of
`capay.holdback-release`.

**Prompt payment runs on a clock that starts with a document.** A proper invoice
starts a 28-day deadline for the owner and a 7-day flow-down to each
subcontractor, and the only lawful way to pay less is a notice of non-payment
given in the prescribed form and time. Interest runs automatically when a
deadline is missed, whether or not anybody asks for it. The policy must name the
position that owns each of those clocks, because a deadline nobody owns is a
deadline that is discovered in a demand letter.

**What this policy must not do is set out the arithmetic of substantial
performance.** That is in the Act, it changes when the Act changes, and a policy
that restates it acquires a second home for a fact and goes stale quietly.
Cite it; do not copy it.
